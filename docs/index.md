<h1 id="hero-title">WHITE LIES</h1>
<p class="wl-hero-subtitle">Is the First-Move Advantage Real?</p>

<section class="wl-split wl-divider" aria-labelledby="question-title">
<div class="wl-reveal"><p class="wl-eyebrow"><span class="wl-section-number">01</span> The questions</p><h2 id="question-title">Two questions about the first moves.</h2><p class="wl-lede">First, does White earn more than half the available points? Second, after White plays 1.e4 or 1.d4, are outcomes different depending on Black's reply?</p><p>A win earns 1 point, a draw earns ½ point, and a loss earns 0. White's score includes draws, so it is different from White's win rate.</p><a class="wl-text-link" href="research/question/">Start with the questions →</a></div>
<div class="wl-duality wl-reveal"><div class="wl-side wl-side-white"><span class="wl-piece" aria-hidden="true">♙</span><div><strong>White</strong><small>Moves first.</small><p>Does White earn<br>more points?</p></div></div><div class="wl-side wl-side-black"><span class="wl-piece" aria-hidden="true">♟</span><div><strong>Black</strong><small>Chooses a reply.</small><p>Are some replies linked<br>to different outcomes?</p></div></div></div>
</section>
<section class="wl-divider" aria-labelledby="method-title"><p class="wl-eyebrow"><span class="wl-section-number">02</span> The approach</p><h2 id="method-title">From games to an answer.</h2><ol class="wl-process"><li><h3>Collect games</h3><p>Use the March 2025 Lichess archive of rated games.</p></li><li><h3>Keep a checked sample</h3><p>Study 836,070 eligible blitz, rapid and classical games.</p></li><li><h3>Adjust the mix</h3><p>Use weights because some game groups were sampled more often.</p></li><li><h3>Compare results</h3><p>Measure White's score and compare Black's opening replies.</p></li><li><h3>Check the findings</h3><p>Try different rating and game-length limits, and measure uncertainty.</p></li></ol><a class="wl-text-link" href="research/approach/">How we studied the questions →</a></section>
<section class="wl-split wl-finding" aria-labelledby="h1-title">
<div class="wl-reveal"><p class="wl-eyebrow"><span class="wl-section-number">03</span> Finding one</p><h2 id="h1-title">White has a small scoring edge.</h2><p class="wl-lede">White's weighted score was <strong>51.776%</strong>: about <strong>51.8 points per 100 games</strong>. The 95% confidence interval, <strong>51.653%–51.899%</strong>, sits entirely above 50%.</p><p>The positive direction holds across the reported checks, but the size changes. Keeping only games lasting at least 40 individual moves reduces the score to 50.769%.</p><a class="wl-text-link" href="research/h1/">Does White score more? →</a></div>

<section class="wl-split" aria-labelledby="h2-title">
<div class="wl-reveal"><p class="wl-eyebrow"><span class="wl-section-number">04</span> Finding two</p><h2 id="h2-title">Black's replies show a less consistent pattern.</h2><p class="wl-lede">We compared replies within the same first move, game speed and rating band. Some groups showed differences in White's score or in how often each outcome occurred.</p><p>When players had closely matched ratings, the evidence became much weaker. The study cannot tell us which defense is best.</p><a class="wl-text-link" href="research/h2/">Does Black's reply matter? →</a></div>

<section class="wl-caveats" aria-labelledby="caveats-title"><div class="wl-caveat-box"><p class="wl-eyebrow"><span class="wl-section-number">05</span> Checks and limits</p><h2 id="caveats-title">What the study can tell us.</h2><ul class="wl-caveat-list"><li><strong>One month, one platform</strong>The findings describe selected Lichess games from March 2025.</li><li><strong>Players make choices</strong>Ratings and opening choices can help explain the patterns. These games do not prove cause and effect.</li><li><strong>Some players return</strong>Games involving the same player may be related.</li><li><strong>The sample matters</strong>Removing short games changes White's score. Closely matching ratings weakens the reply evidence.</li></ul><a class="wl-text-link" href="research/limitations/">Read the checks and limits →</a></div></section>
