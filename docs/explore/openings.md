# Explore Black's first reply

Choose White's first move, a game speed and a rating band. Then choose a reply to show it on the board and see the results for that group.

For example, `1.e4 c5` means White moved the king's pawn to e4 and Black replied with the c-pawn to c5. `Nf6` means a knight moved to f6.

The board shows the moves used to group games. It does not judge the position or recommend a defense.

## Read the two test results

- **Score differences:** do White's average points differ between replies?
- **Win/draw/loss differences:** does the mix of outcomes differ, even if average scores are similar?

In the main analysis, **14 of 35 groups** showed score differences and **15 of 35** showed win/draw/loss differences after adjustment for multiple tests. The displayed adjusted p-values, or q-values, belong to the **whole group of replies**. Selecting a reply does not turn them into a test of that move.

## Which replies appear?

A comparison group fixes White's first move, game speed and the average rating band of the two players. The main analysis uses bands starting at 1200, at least **200 sampled games per reply**, and at least **two eligible replies**.

The table starts in alphabetical move order. Search for a move, select a column heading to sort, or select a reply to show it on the board. Each confidence interval applies to one reply; sorting by score does not establish a reliable ranking.

## Why be cautious?

Different players choose different replies. Among players whose ratings were within 25 points, only **1 of 28 groups** still showed score differences. That check also had fewer games. We cannot separate the effect of reduced data from changes in who is being compared.
